Don't worry. They will let you know.

And won't they just. In a 2,000-word blog post. On a website they coded themselves. With a disclaimer in the footer that no part of it was generated, assisted, or even glanced at by an LLM. And they'll toot about it. Twice.

> *"I make up my own mind about posts I like. Sometimes AI-assisted posts are very good. Maybe it was just a polish. Maybe it was structural reorganisation using AI to make the text flow better. Sometimes you spot the AI tics, sometimes not. I don’t care. If a post has good ideas and good arguments behind them, they’re valuable to me, no matter how they were created. I don’t need a machine to tell me about it. I wouldn’t care if a writer was using a spellchecker to hide that they can’t spell."*

Like Cedric I do not need a machine to tell me what I should like. Neither do I have any time for self-appointed bipedal carbon-based LLM detectors. 

This is not to deny all the known wrongs associated with LLM's and other types of AI. However, individual and collective action will always make more of a difference than preaching, finger pointing and calling out.
